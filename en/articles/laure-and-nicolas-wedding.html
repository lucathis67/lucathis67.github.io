Between the ceremony and the celebration

The ceremony was behind them, the celebration still ahead. In between, I went with Laure and Nicolas for a few portraits around the Palais Rohan. A few steps, just long enough for a handful of pictures, at once classic and full of spontaneous tenderness. In the story of their wedding, these photographs hold on to a rather special moment: the moment when you have just got married, and you take the time to find each other again and simply look at one another.

Slowing down the pace of the big day

When you are planning your wedding, you think a lot about the people who will be there. About welcoming them, about seeing them again, about everything you would like to share with them. On the day itself, between the congratulations, the conversations and the different stages of the day, the chances to spend a few moments together can be rarer than you imagined.

A couple session can give you that time. Stepping away a little, walking side by side, sharing a few words about what you have just lived. After preparing this day for months, also taking the time to experience it together.

Being guided, quite simply

You don’t need to be used to the camera to enjoy this moment. I like to start simply: I find a spot slightly out of the way where the light suits portraits, and I suggest that you move closer, walk a little or look at each other.

I guide you so that you know what to do, then I leave room for whatever comes naturally from you. A hand that comes to rest, a smile answering the other’s, a familiar way of standing together. With Laure and Nicolas, these small gestures give the photographs all their truth.

When in the course of the wedding day?

You might want these pictures while still being reluctant to leave your guests. It is something you can take into account from the very start of your planning, by choosing a nearby location and a moment that fits in with the flow of the day.

Every wedding is unique, and part of my work is adapting to what makes yours special. I like to talk it through with you beforehand: thinking ahead about getting from place to place, the light and the time available, so that this pause can be enjoyed calmly, without having to keep an eye on the clock with every photograph. For Laure and Nicolas, this session slipped in between the ceremony and the celebration, at the Palais Rohan in Strasbourg, a stone’s throw from the Cathedral where the ceremony had taken place.

Remembering your way of being together

In a wedding story, each photograph tells part of the day. Loved ones gathered together, the gestures of the ceremony, the joy of the celebration. Couple portraits bring something more intimate: the way you look at each other and draw close, at this precise moment in a story that is, above all, your own.

I believe these photos grow more precious with the years. Looking back at them, beyond a record of how elegant you both were, you may recognise that smile you know so well, that way of holding each other. Along with the memory of the wedding, you will also keep the memory of the couple you were that day, at the beginning of this new chapter of your life.

Making time for this moment for yourselves

If you are already imagining your wedding day, you can start asking yourselves this question: when would you like to have a few moments, just the two of you?

Talk about it together, then with your photographer. They can help you find a place for this moment, taking into account what matters to you and the rhythm of your day. As with Laure and Nicolas, it may be between the ceremony and the celebration: a few steps together, a moment suspended in time, before you rejoin the people you love.
